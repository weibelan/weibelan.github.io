I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Hawaiʻi at Manoa, studying distant galaxies and black holes. My main interests are in the identification and characterization of galaxies at high redshift from photometric data -- mostly using the James Webb and Hubble Space Telescopes. My PhD thesis at the University of Geneva has been awarded the [Prix Schläfli 2026](https://scnat.ch/en/awards/schlaefli/uuid/i/2304ab78-5b45-5965-88f3-f812be43ff31-Andrea_Weibel_discovered_that_galaxies_grow_up_surprisingly_quickly) for one of the four best dissertations in natural sciences in Switzerland.

## Research

Having started my PhD about 8 months before the first JWST data became available, I made some of the first JWST photometric catalogs which have contributed to the discovery of the most distant galaxies known to date, as well as the 
so-called Little Red Dots. Based on those catalogs, I have published [stellar mass functions](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.533.1808W/abstract) and [UV luminosity functions](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1002..136W/abstract) at high redshift that constitute benchmarks in the field. In a 
follow-up project to the UV-LF, we have explored a complementary way of constraining galaxy physics at the redshift frontier through measurements of [cosmic variance at z = 10](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1005..118W/abstract). 
I have been involved in various spectroscopic follow-up programs with JWST, most notably [RUBIES](https://annadeg.github.io/projects/RUBIES/). Selected for follow-up from my photometric catalogs, I have published the spectrum of the [most distant massive quiescent galaxy](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...11W/abstract), observed only 700 million years after the Big Bang.  
More recently, my focus has shifted to Little Red Dots, specifically their selection from JWST imaging, including their [pure engines](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260617271W/abstract) (so-called Black Hole Stars). Currently, I am extending this search to lower redshifts using ground-based imaging data to find exciting targets
for spectroscopic follow-up from the ground, using the [Keck telescope](https://keckobservatory.org/) at Maunakea.
