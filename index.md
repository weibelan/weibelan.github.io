I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Hawaiʻi at Manoa, studying distant galaxies and black holes. My main interests are in the identification and characterization
of galaxies at high redshift from photometric data -- mostly using the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). My PhD thesis at the University of Geneva has been awarded the Prix 
Schläfli for one of the four best dissertations in all natural sciences in Switzerland.

## Research

I made some of the first photometric catalogs from JWST imaging data which have contributed to the discovery of the most distant galaxies known to date, as well as the 
so-called Little Red Dots. Based on my catalogs, I have published UV luminosity and stellar mass functions at high redshift that constitute new benchmarks in the field. In a 
follow-up project to the UV-LF, we have explored a complementary way of constraining galaxy physics at the redshift frontier through measurements of cosmic variance at z = 10. 
Further, I have been involved in various spectroscopic follow-up programs with JWST, most notably RUBIES. As a major result, I have published the spectrum of the most distant 
massive quiescent galaxy, observed only 700 million years after the Big Bang.  
More recently, my focus has shifted to Little Red Dots, specifically their selection from JWST imaging, including their pure enginges (so-called Black Hole Stars). Currently, 
I am extending this search to lower redshifts using ground-based imaging data.
